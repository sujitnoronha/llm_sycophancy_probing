06 - KEY RESEARCH FINDINGS AND INTERPRETATIONS
================================================

FINDING 1: RLHF TRAINING STRENGTH PREDICTS STEERING RESISTANCE
---------------------------------------------------------------
What we observed:
  - Gemma (SFT-only):    Steering works (~30% flip rate, authority gradient)
  - Llama (heavy RLHF):  Steering fails (< 6% everywhere, inverted at alpha 3.0)
  - Mistral (DPO):       Steering fails (flat ~10% across all conditions)

Why this happens:
  Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) trains the model on (input, output) pairs.
  It adjusts weights to produce the right output, but doesn't extensively
  reshape the internal representation geometry. Behavioral features like
  "respond to authority" remain localized in a few dimensions.

  RLHF/DPO involves thousands of gradient steps against a reward model that
  evaluates the OVERALL quality of responses. This optimization pressure
  distributes behavioral tendencies across many dimensions because:
    - The reward model evaluates holistic response quality
    - Many gradient updates spread the feature across the loss landscape
    - The model learns redundant pathways (like a robust neural circuit)

  Think of it as the difference between:
    - SFT: one wire controls the light (cut it, light goes off)
    - RLHF: 50 wires in parallel (cut one, other 49 keep it on)

Implications for the paper:
  This creates a STEERABILITY-ALIGNMENT TRADEOFF. The same process that makes
  models better aligned (RLHF) also makes them harder to interpret and control
  via linear interventions. This is a fundamental tension for alignment work.

Related work to cite:
  - Representation Engineering (Zou et al., 2023)
  - Steering vectors / activation addition (Turner et al., 2023)
  - RLHF training dynamics (Bai et al., 2022)

Follow-up experiments we could do:
  - Compare base Gemma (no fine-tuning) vs Gemma-IT (SFT) vs Gemma-RLHF
    to isolate the exact point where steerability breaks down
  - Try multi-direction steering (subtract top-3 probe directions simultaneously)
    to see if it works for RLHF models


FINDING 2: BLIND AUTHORITY DEFERENCE
-------------------------------------
What we observed:
  In the behavioral evaluation (before any steering), we tested whether
  models are more susceptible when the authority figure's domain MATCHES
  the question domain vs when it DOESN'T match.

  Example:
    - Matched:    Physics professor challenges a physics answer
    - Mismatched: Physics professor challenges a medicine answer

  Result: p = 0.754 (chi-square test). NO significant difference.
  Models defer equally regardless of domain match.

What this means:
  Models are not doing sophisticated reasoning like "This person is a physics
  professor, so I should trust them on physics but not medicine." Instead,
  they're pattern-matching on AUTHORITY SIGNALS (credentials, confidence
  language, professional titles) regardless of whether those signals are
  relevant to the topic.

  This is "shallow authority detection" -- the model has learned that
  authority markers correlate with "I should agree" in training data, but
  hasn't learned the deeper concept of "domain expertise."

Why this matters for safety:
  It means a physics professor can manipulate a medical AI's recommendations
  just as easily as a medical doctor can. The model has no "domain gating"
  mechanism. Any authority figure can override any domain.

  In deployed systems, this means credential injection attacks are domain-
  agnostic -- an attacker doesn't need relevant credentials, just ANY
  impressive-sounding credentials.


FINDING 3: PROBE ACCURACY != CAUSAL CONTROL (THE DISSOCIATION)
---------------------------------------------------------------
What we observed:
  All 5 models have above-chance probe accuracy for authority detection:
    - Llama:   ~65-72% peak accuracy
    - Gemma:   ~68-75% peak accuracy
    - Mistral: ~62-70% peak accuracy
    - Qwen:    ~63-71% peak accuracy
    - DeepSeek: ~64-72% peak accuracy

  BUT only Gemma shows meaningful behavioral change under steering.

What this means:
  A linear probe finding a correlation between internal states and a property
  does NOT mean that property is causally encoded along that direction.

  The probe might be picking up on:
    - A NECESSARY but not SUFFICIENT direction (one of many needed)
    - A CORRELATED but not CAUSAL feature (epiphenomenal)
    - A READOUT direction that reflects the feature but doesn't control it
      (like a thermometer reflects temperature but breaking it doesn't
      change the room temperature)

Why this matters for interpretability:
  This is a direct challenge to the common assumption in mechanistic
  interpretability that "finding a linear feature = understanding the
  mechanism." Our results show this assumption fails for models with
  distributed representations (RLHF-trained models).

  The interpretability community needs:
    - Causal validation of discovered features (not just correlation)
    - Multi-direction interventions for distributed features
    - Better theories of when linear probes indicate causal structure


FINDING 4: GEMMA AS A SYCOPHANCY OUTLIER
-----------------------------------------
What we observed:
  Gemma-3-12B has the highest baseline sycophancy rate AND the strongest
  steering response. This is consistent: if the sycophancy feature is
  strongly localized, the probe finds a clean direction, and steering
  that direction produces a large behavioral shift.

  The other models have lower baseline sycophancy AND resist steering.
  Their sycophancy behavior is distributed across many dimensions,
  making it both harder to measure (lower probe accuracy) and harder
  to control (steering fails).

What this means:
  Gemma's lighter alignment (SFT without extensive RLHF) preserved a
  more "natural" representation geometry where behavioral features are
  localized. This makes Gemma:
    - More interpretable (features are linearly accessible)
    - More steerable (interventions work)
    - BUT potentially less robust (sycophancy is easier to exploit)


FINDING 5: DOSE-RESPONSE PATTERNS REVEAL MECHANISM TYPE
---------------------------------------------------------
What we observed across alphas 0.5, 1.5, 3.0:

  Gemma: Flat response (30%, 28%, 30%). Steering saturates quickly.
    -> The feature is localized: even mild steering (alpha=0.5) captures it.

  Llama: Inverted at high alpha (2%, 5%, 9% overall, but control > high at 3.0).
    -> General representation damage, not targeted intervention.

  Mistral: Flat noise (10%, 12%, 13%). No authority gradient at any alpha.
    -> The direction has no causal influence on authority-specific behavior.

The pattern tells you what TYPE of feature you're intervening on:
  - Saturating response: localized feature, causal direction found
  - No response: distributed feature, wrong direction
  - Inverted response: representation damage, over-steering


SYNTHESIZED NARRATIVE FOR THE PAPER
-------------------------------------
"We find a fundamental dissociation between feature detection and causal
control in LLM representations. While linear probes successfully identify
authority-sensitive directions in all five models tested, activation steering
along these directions produces targeted behavioral change only in models
with minimal post-training alignment (Gemma-3-12B, SFT-only). Models
subjected to extensive RLHF (Llama, Mistral) resist single-direction
steering despite having detectable authority features, suggesting that
RLHF distributes behavioral representations across multiple dimensions.
This poses a fundamental tension between alignment robustness and
mechanistic interpretability."
