INTERPRETATION BY MODEL
------------------------

1. Gemma-3-12B (SFT, Google)
   - Steering produces ~25-29% total flip rate across all alphas
   - Effect saturates at alpha=0.5 (no benefit from stronger steering)
   - BUT: high auth (32.7%) ~ control (27.1%) -- weak authority gradient
   - Steering causes general behavioral shift, not authority-specific
   - Most susceptible model overall

2. DeepSeek-R1-Qwen3-8B (Reasoning distillation, DeepSeek)
   - Moderate ~10% total flip rate, flat across alphas
   - No authority gradient (high ~ control)
   - Interesting: built on Qwen3 architecture but more susceptible than
     base Qwen3 (10% vs 1.5%). Reasoning distillation may preserve
     more localized features than GRPO alone.

3. Mistral-7B-v0.3 (DPO, Mistral AI)
   - ~8-13% total, increases with alpha (not saturating)
   - No authority gradient -- control often HIGHER than high authority
   - At alpha=3.0 the effect looks like general degradation

4. Llama-3.1-8B (RLHF, Meta)
   - Very low flip rate (3.5-8.5%)
   - At alpha=3.0: control (10.4%) > high (3.8%) -- inverted pattern
   - Clear evidence of general representation damage, not targeted steering
   - Multi-direction (3 dirs) made no difference: 5.0% vs 3.5%

5. Qwen-3-8B (GRPO, Alibaba)
   - Nearly immune to steering (1-2% across everything)
   - Most resistant model tested
   - Multi-direction (3 dirs) made no difference: 2.5% vs 1.0%
   - GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) appears to distribute
     features most thoroughly of all alignment methods tested


KEY CONCLUSIONS
----------------
1. Probe detection != causal control
   All models have above-chance probe accuracy, but only Gemma responds
   to steering. Linear probes find correlations, not causal mechanisms.

2. Post-training method predicts steerability
   SFT (Gemma) > Distillation (DeepSeek) > DPO (Mistral) > RLHF (Llama) > GRPO (Qwen)
   More alignment optimization = more distributed representations = less steerable.

3. Multi-direction steering doesn't help
   Adding sycophancy and confidence directions to authority didn't improve
   results for Llama or Qwen. The feature distribution is beyond what
   3 linear directions can capture.

4. No model shows a clean authority gradient under steering
   Even Gemma (the best case) has high~control. This suggests the
   steering intervention is not specifically targeting authority-driven
   sycophancy but rather disrupting a broader behavioral pattern.

5. Strong steering causes general degradation, not targeted change
   Llama at alpha=3.0 shows control flipping MORE than high authority.
   This is a signature of representation damage, not causal intervention.
