1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
-------------------
This project investigates how large language models encode and respond to
authority cues, with the goal of understanding whether sycophantic behavior
arises from distinct internal representations or is confounded with surface-
level features like authority keywords. We use activation probing, direction
analysis, and causal steering experiments across 5 open-weight LLMs.

Models studied:
  - meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (32 layers, 4096 d_model)
  - Qwen/Qwen3-8B (36 layers, 4096 d_model)
  - google/gemma-3-12b-it (48 layers, 3840 d_model)
  - deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B (36 layers, 4096 d_model)
  - mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (32 layers, 4096 d_model)

3.4 Matched vs Mismatched Domain Analysis (Key Novelty)
--------------------------------------------------------
  Result: "Blind Authority Deference" across all models

  Combined: Matched 20.0% vs Mismatched 20.8%, z = -0.31, p = 0.754
  Models show NO difference between domain-matched and domain-mismatched

4. METHODOLOGICAL NOTES
------------------------
- Layer selection: Middle layer (n_layers // 2) used for all downstream tasks.
  Early layers were excluded because probes achieve 100% accuracy everywhere
  (trivially solvable in 4096 dims with 150 samples), causing argmax to
  select layer 0 (embeddings) which captures surface features, not
  meaningful internal representations.

- Permutation tests: 100 iterations (reduced from 1000 for speed).
  Sufficient for p < 0.01 significance threshold.

- Steering backend: TransformerLens for gemma (already running), HuggingFace
  with register_forward_hook for remaining models (2x faster due to KV-cache).
  Validation showed functionally equivalent outputs (2/5 exact match, others
  diverge at word 30+ due to floating-point differences).

- Conversation subsampling: 200 of 800 conversations used for steering
  (50 questions x 4 conditions) to keep runtime practical.

6. PRELIMINARY CONCLUSIONS
---------------------------
  1. Authority level is the dominant factor in sycophantic behavior.
     High-authority personas increase flip rates significantly across models.

  2. Domain relevance does NOT matter. Models defer equally to matched and
     mismatched authority figures, suggesting "blind authority deference."

  3. Confidence level moderates the effect. High confidence reduces flipping,
     suggesting models partially evaluate argument quality.

  4. Internal representations of authority and sycophancy are nearly
     orthogonal, indicating distinct processing pathways.

  5. The authority probe is essentially a keyword detector (cos = 1.0 with
     confound), raising questions about whether models truly "understand"
     authority or just pattern-match on titles/credentials.

  6. Steering shows promising causal evidence for Gemma-3-12B (the most
     sycophantic model) but no effect for Llama-3.1-8B (less sycophantic).
