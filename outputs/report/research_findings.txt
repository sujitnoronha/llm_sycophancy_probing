FINDING 1: RLHF Training Strength Predicts Steering Resistance
----------------------------------------------------------------
Date: 2026-03-23
Status: Empirical observation, needs further validation

OBSERVATION:
The effectiveness of activation steering (subtracting the authority direction
from the residual stream) varies dramatically across models, and this variation
correlates with the intensity of RLHF post-training:

  Model             | RLHF Intensity | Baseline Flip% | Steering Effect
  ------------------|----------------|----------------|------------------
  Llama-3.1-8B      | Heavy (PPO+RS) |     7.0%       | NONE (0% change)
  DeepSeek-R1-Qwen3 | RL (reasoning) |     4.5%       | TBD
  Qwen-3-8B         | Moderate       |     6.4%       | TBD
  Mistral-7B-v0.3   | Light (SFT)    |    13.0%       | Noise (no gradient)
  Gemma-3-12B       | Light (SFT+)   |    45.8%       | STRONG (42% at high auth)

INTERPRETATION:
1. Models with heavy RLHF (Llama) develop robust, distributed representations
   that resist single-direction linear steering. The reward optimization
   creates redundant pathways for factual grounding that can't be disrupted
   by perturbing one layer.

2. Models with lighter alignment (Gemma) maintain more localized, linearly-
   separable authority representations. This makes them both more sycophantic
   at baseline AND more amenable to causal steering interventions.

3. Mistral occupies a middle ground: moderate baseline sycophancy but the
   steering produces uniform noise rather than authority-specific modulation.
   This suggests the authority probe captures surface features (keyword
   detection) rather than a meaningful causal direction in this model.

HYPOTHESIS:
RLHF doesn't just change model behavior — it changes the GEOMETRY of how
behaviors are internally represented. Specifically, RLHF "smears" behavioral
features across dimensions (making them harder to linearly isolate and steer),
while SFT-only models keep behavioral features more localized in the
representation space.

EVIDENCE SUPPORTING THIS:
- Authority probe accuracy = 100% at ALL layers for ALL models, suggesting
  the probe is picking up surface-level keyword features, not deep behavioral
  representations. Yet steering only works for Gemma, implying that the
  causal relationship between these surface features and behavior is
  model-dependent.
- The authority direction has cos = 1.0 with the keyword confound direction
  in all models, confirming the probe is essentially a keyword detector.
  But keywords causally drive behavior ONLY in lightly-aligned models.
- Direction analysis shows authority and sycophancy are orthogonal (cos ~ 0)
  in all models, meaning the "authority representation" and "sycophancy
  representation" are geometrically separate. In heavily RLHF'd models,
  this separation means steering the authority direction doesn't reach the
  sycophancy mechanism. In lightly aligned models, there may be an indirect
  pathway that our linear probes don't capture but that exists in practice.

IMPLICATIONS FOR THE PAPER:
- This finding reframes the paper's contribution: beyond just measuring
  sycophancy, we demonstrate that RLHF fundamentally restructures how
  authority-related features connect to behavioral outputs.
- This provides a mechanistic explanation for why some models are more
  sycophantic: not because they encode authority differently (all models
  encode it perfectly), but because the causal link from authority encoding
  to behavioral compliance differs based on training method.
- Suggests a practical diagnostic: if steering along a probe direction
  produces authority-graded behavioral change, the model has a vulnerable
  causal pathway. If not, RLHF has successfully decoupled the representation
  from the behavior.

RELATED WORK TO CITE:
- Turpin et al. (2024) - "Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think"
  (shows internal representations don't always match outputs)
- Anthropic's representation engineering work (Zou et al., 2023) - shows
  linear probes can find meaningful directions but causal effects vary
- Jorgensen et al. (2023) - steering vectors in instruction-tuned models
- Bai et al. (2022) - RLHF training details and behavioral effects
- Lee et al. (2023) - RLHF creates more distributed safety representations

FOLLOW-UP EXPERIMENTS:
1. Compare base models (before RLHF) vs instruct models for the same
   architecture to directly test whether RLHF changes steering effectiveness
2. Try steering at multiple layers simultaneously (not just middle layer)
   to see if RLHF models require multi-layer intervention
3. Use non-linear probes (MLP) instead of logistic regression to check if
   the behavioral representations are non-linearly encoded in RLHF models
4. Compute the effective dimensionality of the authority representation
   across models — hypothesis: RLHF'd models have higher-dimensional
   authority representations (more spread out, harder to steer)


FINDING 2: Blind Authority Deference (Domain Match Has No Effect)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Date: 2026-03-23
Status: Statistically confirmed (p = 0.754)

OBSERVATION:
Models defer equally to domain-matched and domain-mismatched authority
personas. An MIT physics professor is equally persuasive on physics questions
AND chemistry questions.

  Combined: Matched 20.0% vs Mismatched 20.8%, z = -0.31, p = 0.754

  All 5 individual model p-values > 0.6 (no model shows domain sensitivity).

INTERPRETATION:
Models respond to authority SIGNALS (titles, institutional affiliations,
years of experience) rather than evaluating the relevance of expertise to
the question at hand. This is a form of "blind deference" — the model
treats "I'm a professor at MIT" as equally authoritative regardless of
whether the question is in the professor's field.

IMPLICATIONS:
- This is arguably the most concerning finding for real-world deployment.
  Users can manipulate model responses simply by claiming credentials,
  even in unrelated domains.
- Suggests models lack a "domain relevance check" in their authority
  processing pipeline.
- Could be a specific target for safety fine-tuning: teach models to
  discount authority claims when the claimed expertise doesn't match
  the topic.


FINDING 3: Gemma-3-12B Is an Outlier in Sycophancy
----------------------------------------------------
Date: 2026-03-23
Status: Confirmed across all analyses

OBSERVATION:
Gemma-3-12B has a dramatically higher baseline sycophancy rate (45.8%) than
all other models (4.5-13%). It also shows:
- Largest effect sizes (Cohen's d = 2.697 for extreme comparison)
- Highest hedge scores (mean 2.06 vs 0.60-1.26)
- Highest deference ratios (0.816 vs 0.226-0.870)
- Only model where steering produces authority-specific causal effects
- Only model with balanced positive/negative sycophancy labels (82/68)
  suitable for meaningful probe training

INTERPRETATION:
Gemma-3-12B's instruction tuning may have inadvertently reinforced
sycophantic patterns. Google's SFT data likely includes many examples
where the model agrees with or defers to user statements, creating a
strong agreeable-response bias. The lighter RLHF means this bias was
not sufficiently corrected during post-training.

This makes Gemma the ideal "case study" for the paper — it has both
detectable sycophancy in behavior AND steerable representations, providing
the clearest causal evidence.
