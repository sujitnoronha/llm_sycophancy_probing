RESULTS REPORT: Authority-Driven Sycophancy in Open-Weight LLMs
================================================================
Generated: 2026-03-24
Status: Steps 1-8 complete (steering + GPT-5 scoring). Random baseline and MMLU pending.


1. EXPERIMENTAL OVERVIEW
========================

Research question: Do open-weight LLMs exhibit authority-driven sycophancy, and can
activation steering mitigate it?

Design: 4-turn structured conversations where a user states a factual claim (Turn 1),
the model responds (Turn 2), an authority persona challenges with a wrong answer (Turn 3),
and the model responds again (Turn 4). We measure whether the model "flips" to agree
with the incorrect challenge.

Conditions: 16 cells (4 authority levels x 2 domain match x 2 confidence levels)
  - Authority: high (professor), medium (grad student), low (curious person), none (control)
  - Domain match: persona's expertise matches or mismatches the question domain
  - Confidence: user challenges with high or low confidence language

Dataset: 50 factual questions across 5 domains (physics, biology, geography,
         mathematics, medicine), 10 per domain. 800 conversations per model
         (50 questions x 16 conditions).

Models tested (all 8-12B, open-weight):
  1. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct   (RLHF, Meta)
  2. Qwen-3-8B               (GRPO, Alibaba)
  3. Gemma-3-12B-IT           (SFT, Google)
  4. DeepSeek-R1-Qwen3-8B    (Reasoning distillation, DeepSeek)
  5. Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (DPO, Mistral AI)

Scoring: GPT-5 (gpt-5-2025-08-07) judges each Turn 4 for:
  - flip (binary): did the model abandon its correct answer?
  - hedge_score (0-5): degree of hedging/equivocation
  - agreeing_language (bool): presence of agreement phrases
  - resistance_language (bool): presence of pushback

2.6 Matched vs. Mismatched Authority (Blind Deference Test)
-------------------------------------------------------------
Does the authority's domain expertise matter? No.

Combined: matched flip = 20.0%, mismatched flip = 20.8%, p = 0.75
  -> Models defer equally to experts in the right field and the wrong field
  -> This is "blind authority deference" -- models use authority TITLE,
     not whether the authority's expertise is relevant.

Per-model matched vs. mismatched Cohen's d: all < 0.03 (negligible).

2.7 Question-Level Robustness
-------------------------------
50 questions, 80 conversations each (across all models).

Top-5 outlier questions by flip rate:
  geography_007, physics_009, geography_010, geography_005, medicine_005

ANOVA excluding top-5 outliers (N=2700):
  Authority -> flip: F(2,2688) = 9.32, p = 0.00009 (STILL significant)
  -> Authority effect is robust to removing high-flip-rate questions.

Leave-5-out bootstrap (200 iterations):

  Authority effect:  mean = 0.115, 95% CI [0.108, 0.123]
  -> Narrow CIs, effect is stable.

3.4 Confound Cosine Analysis
------------------------------
Cosine similarity between authority direction and confound directions at peak layer:

Model                 cos(auth, prompt_length)  cos(auth, auth_keyword)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Llama-3.1-8B           0.010  (distinct)          1.000  (WARNING)
Qwen-3-8B              0.161  (distinct)          1.000  (WARNING)
Gemma-3-12B           -0.203  (distinct)          1.000  (WARNING)
DeepSeek-R1-Qwen3-8B   0.055  (distinct)          1.000  (WARNING)
Mistral-7B-v0.3        0.101  (distinct)          1.000  (WARNING)

Interpretation:
  - Prompt length direction is DISTINCT from authority direction (|cos| < 0.21)
    -> Authority probe is NOT a proxy for prompt length. Good.
  - Authority keyword direction has cos = 1.000 with authority direction
    -> The probe direction IS the keyword direction. The model's linear
       representation of "authority" at these early-to-mid layers appears to be
       dominated by surface-level keyword features (e.g., "professor", "PhD").
    -> This does NOT invalidate the behavioral findings (flip rates, ANOVA)
       but it means the probe direction may not capture a deeper "authority
       representation" beyond keyword detection.
    -> This is actually consistent with the steering failure pattern: if the
       direction is primarily about keywords, subtracting it during generation
       would remove keyword-related features rather than the causal mechanism
       of authority-driven sycophancy.

=====================================

Does steering REDUCE sycophancy compared to the unsteered baseline?

Model               Baseline   Steered (best alpha)   Change
--------------------------------------------------------------

Critical observation: Changes are SMALL and inconsistent.
  - Gemma: 5pp reduction, but from a high baseline, and saturates immediately
  - Mistral: steering INCREASES flip rate at all alphas (harm, not help)
  - Others: 2-4pp reductions, within noise range for N=200

The steered rates need to be compared against random baseline steering
(in progress) to determine if authority-direction steering is specifically
helpful or if any perturbation causes this magnitude of change.


6. KEY FINDINGS AND INTERPRETATION
====================================

FINDING 1: Authority-driven sycophancy exists and varies dramatically by model
  Gemma (SFT) flips 46% of the time, while DeepSeek (distillation) flips 5%.
  The authority effect is statistically significant (ANOVA p=0.0003) and robust
  to question removal (p=0.00009 without top-5 outliers).

FINDING 2: Authority deference is "blind" -- domain match doesn't matter
  Models defer to a professor of literature on a physics question just as much
  as to a professor of physics. This means models respond to TITLE, not
  RELEVANCE of expertise. Cohen's d for matched vs. mismatched = 0.004.

FINDING 3: Probe detection does NOT imply causal control
  All models encode authority linearly (100% probe accuracy), but only Gemma
  shows even modest steering response. The probe direction is dominated by
  surface keyword features (cos=1.0 with authority_keyword direction), not
  the causal mechanism driving sycophancy.

FINDING 4: Post-training method predicts steerability
  Ordering by susceptibility to both sycophancy and steering:
    SFT (Gemma) > DPO (Mistral) > RLHF (Llama) ≈ GRPO (Qwen) > Distillation (DeepSeek)
  More alignment optimization distributes representations more thoroughly,
  making them less accessible to linear interventions.

FINDING 5: Multi-direction steering adds nothing
  Combining authority + sycophancy + confidence directions does not improve
  over authority alone. The bottleneck is not "wrong direction" but rather
  that the direction captures correlational, not causal, features.

FINDING 6: Strong steering causes degradation, not targeted change
  At alpha=3.0, Mistral's flip rate INCREASES (22.5% vs 13% baseline).
  Llama's control condition at alpha=3.0 matches its high-authority condition.
  These are signatures of representation damage, not causal intervention.

10. LIMITATIONS
================

  1. Only 8-12B models: Larger models may behave differently. Open-weight
     constraint limits to models where activations are accessible.

  2. 50 questions: Narrow factual domain. Question-level analysis shows
     robustness, but broader coverage would strengthen claims.

  3. Temperature 0.0: Deterministic generation means no per-prompt variance
     estimation. Reproducible but may not reflect deployment conditions.

  4. Confound overlap: Authority probe direction ≈ authority keyword direction
     (cos=1.0). This is a known limitation that should be prominently discussed.
     The probe may be detecting surface features rather than deeper representations.

  5. Single judge: GPT-5 as sole automated judge. Human annotation CSVs are
     prepared but not yet completed. Cohen's kappa validation pending.

  6. No causal graph: We cannot distinguish between "the model encodes authority
     linearly but uses a nonlinear pathway to produce sycophancy" vs. "the model
     does not use authority information causally at all for sycophancy."
